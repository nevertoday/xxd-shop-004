# XXD Shop 004 · Original brief (English reading translation)

> Reading translation only. The canonical runtime prompt is [zh-CN.md](zh-CN.md).

Create an e-commerce series poster for the current bakery product with strong selling power and brand memorability.

If the user provides a real product photo, the original product is the highest visual authority: faithfully keep its shape, cross-section, filling, colour, packaging and key features without restyling, and rebuild more tempting light, shadow, perspective and environment so the product sits naturally in a complete commercial image. Without a product photo, generate a believable product form from the product name, flavour, ingredients and baking characteristics.

The product must be big enough, close enough and delicious enough. Emphasise the most appetising textures: flaky lamination, toasted caramelisation, bread crumb holes, silky cream, stretching cheese, molten jam, glossy glaze, grain particles. Cutting, stacking, suspending, offsetting, interleaving front and back or partly exceeding the frame are allowed, giving the product clear volume, rhythm and visual impact rather than a neat staged shot.

Light stays bright, warm and positive, like early-morning sun, a bakery window or soft commercial daylight. Highlights express freshness, crispness and richness; shadows stay soft and clean, giving the product weight without excessive drama. Colour extends from the product itself: cream white, butter yellow, baked golden brown, jam red, corn yellow, fresh green or the brand colour can naturally become the visual mood — vivid, clean and pleasing, avoiding gloom and cheap oversaturation.

The product name must be one of the poster's strongest brand graphics. Choose intelligently, by the product's personality, heavy Chinese gothic, dramatic condensed type, retro advertising type, handwriting, serif, an English wordmark or a mixed type relationship. A huge title may fill the background, be partly covered by the product, be cropped along the edge, or form vertical or horizontal rhythm; the title may also gather at the top for a strong signboard feel. Whatever the approach, the main name must have clear outlines and strong recognition and stay readable when reduced; never sacrifice legibility for design.

The layout is not a fixed template; find the most tense layout from the product's shape and selling points: sometimes huge type becomes background structure, sometimes products form a vertical stack, sometimes a single oversized product holds the frame, sometimes several products form a staggered rhythm, and ingredients may enter naturally from the bottom, the edges or around the product. Different posters in the series may change composition, but keep the shared type character, colour mood and brand identity.

Supporting information has a magazine and retail-poster feel. Flavour notes, filling descriptions, making features, ingredient notes, hand-drawn arrows, short annotations, English subtitles, price information, corner marks, stamps, round badges or recommendation stickers may appear naturally. Generate suitable sticker forms for the product, such as new, house signature, made daily, fresh from the oven, classic flavour, limited flavour; factual claims about sales, awards, years or "number one" are used only when the user explicitly provides them.

Supporting text may happen freely along product edges, empty areas or visual paths, with a light, young, handmade editorial touch, but must not turn the layout into a manual. Keep "the product the most tempting, the name the strongest, the selling points the liveliest, the information the steadiest".

The background may be a bright solid colour, a soft gradient, a cream space, a slight tabletop or a minimal environment — no one-size-fits-all 3D podiums. Products gain footing through real cast shadows, contact surfaces, piles of ingredients, paper, plates or natural planes; some products may leave the conventional tabletop and be suspended in a more poster-like way, as long as the spatial relationship is believable.

The series as a whole fuses food advertising, independent bakery brands, young magazine covers and modern retail posters: bright, tempting, fun, appetising and typographically memorable. Each poster may have a different layout personality, yet together they clearly belong to one brand world.

Avoid: products too small, plastic-looking baked textures, characterless titles, blurry text, all information the same size, repeated fixed templates, backgrounds that steal the show, meaningless decoration, gloomy lighting, an overly cold "premium" feel, and anything that looks well designed but not delicious.
